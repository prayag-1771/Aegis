4 · Research Lab4:20 – 5:35
On screen: Research Lab tab — Do: play the spectral sound
Say

That's detection working today. Here's what we built to test whether it survives tomorrow.

First problem: criminals deliberately spread accounts across different banks. Each bank sees a quarter of the ring and dismisses it as noise. And by law, banks cannot share customer data to compare notes.

So we built Ghost Ring. Four banks, each training its own AI, sharing no customer data — only an abstract fingerprint, with privacy noise on top.

The best any single bank could see was twenty-three percent of the ring. Fused, forty-nine percent. Two point two times better — with zero false links. And the privacy protection cost us nothing in accuracy.

DROP IF SHORT Second: we let a simulated criminal evolve against our detector for twenty generations. It survives — and where it didn't, we published that too.

Third — and this one you can hear.

(play clean community, then ring)

That's a normal group of accounts. And that's a fraud ring. A criminal network literally vibrates at a different frequency.

Q&A — the answers to have ready

If they askSay
"Does the message go to your server?""No. The classifier runs in the browser — same model, verified score-for-score. You saw it work in aeroplane mode."
"What do you send, then?""Verdict, scam type, district, timestamp. Not the message. That's all the crime map needs."
"How do you stop AI hallucination?""The AI never decides. Every verdict is from a deterministic model. The AI only writes the explanation. Remove it and nothing changes."
"Is this just synthetic data?""The counterfeit model is real photographs. The fraud graph was benchmarked on 265,354 real Bitcoin wallets at 0.9945. The scam model includes a public SMS spam corpus."
"What doesn't work?""Three things. Investment scams — we catch about half; they read like real financial advice. Live-call speech-to-text goes through the browser, so audio reaches Google. And our synthetic fraud numbers are flattering, which is why we benchmarked on real Bitcoin data."
"Could this be deployed?""For SMS yes — Android can read messages, iOS has an on-device SMS filter API for exactly this. For calls, no app on either platform may tap call audio, so that needs telecom-operator integration. Policy constraint, not technical."
"How is this different from Truecaller?""Truecaller flags numbers. We classify content — so we work on first contact from an unknown number, and we monitor the call while it's happening."
"Isn't RBI already doing this?""MuleHunter flags mule accounts per bank. We built that, plus the layer that joins the ring back to the scam that fed it — and a version that works across banks without them sharing customer data."
Three things never to say:
  • "Everything runs on-device" — say "scam analysis is on-device; speech-to-text is the browser's service"
  • "Our AI decides if it's a scam" — say "a deterministic model decides; the AI explains"
  • "100% accurate" — quote the real number, and the category where you're weakest
